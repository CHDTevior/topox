\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

A rig $\mathcal{R}=(\mathcal{T},\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{rest}},\mathcal{D})$ is a kinematic tree
$\mathcal{T}$ over $J$ joints, a rest pose $\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{rest}}\in\mathbb{R}^{J\times3}$ and
a natural-language description $\mathcal{D}=\{d_j\}_{j=1}^{J}$ of its joints. A motion is a tensor
$\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times J\times 17}$ in the \rep{} representation of
Section~\ref{sec:method:rep}. Given $\mathcal{R}$ and a caption $c$, \method{} samples
$\mathbf{x}\sim p_\theta(\mathbf{x}\mid\mathcal{R},c)$ with one transformer shared by every rig
(Section~\ref{sec:method:model}), trained by a calibrated grouped flow-matching objective
(Section~\ref{sec:method:objective}) and sampled in 20 Euler steps
(Section~\ref{sec:method:sampling}). A rig outside the training library is served by the same
forward pass and, when a few clips are available, by a per-rig LoRA
(Section~\ref{sec:method:lora}); all retrieval metrics come from a skeleton-aware evaluator
(Section~\ref{sec:method:eval}).

\subsection{\rep{}: a per-joint representation any rig can be converted to}\label{sec:method:rep}

\paragraph{Channels.} Every joint carries the same 17 channels (Table~\ref{tab:channels} in App.~\ref{app:rep}). Positions
are expressed relative to the root's own ground-plane track, with height absolute, so
that the body's pose and its travel are separated the way animators separate them. Rotations are
continuous 6D~\citep{zhou2019continuity} \emph{deltas} from the joint's rest orientation in the global
frame: ``no motion'' is the identity rotation, $[1,0,0,0,1,0]$ before normalisation, on every rig,
which gives the rest frame one meaning across rigs when it serves as the skeleton prompt
(Section~\ref{sec:method:model}). Velocities are supervision only and never
integrated at decode time and the root comes from the trajectory channels, so every frame decodes on its own,
without accumulated drift, by either of two routes that do not read each other: the position channels, or forward
kinematics from the rotation channels. Their disagreement is the FK--pose gap this paper reports --- a property
the representation exposes, not a metric imposed on it. Everything is expressed in one canonical frame ($+Y$ up, rest forward $+Z$) obtained
by a rigid per-rig recanonicalisation to the foot support plane, after which forward kinematics is
unchanged to numerical precision (App.~\ref{app:rep}). Building \rep{} for a new rig needs only its
kinematic tree, rest pose and animation: there is no template skeleton, no retargeting and no
inverse-kinematics fitting, and the same channel definition and codec convert every
Truebones rig used in Section~\ref{sec:method:lora}.

\paragraph{Per-cell normalisation.} Rigs differ in size, proportion and convention by orders of
magnitude, so every (rig, joint, channel) cell is standardised with its own mean and standard
deviation ($\sigma$ floored at 0.05), and cells that are constant across a rig's library
(\num{9{,}123} never-firing contact flags, for instance) are removed from supervision. The
statistics are a property of the rig, computed from its motion library and supplied with it at
deployment, the footing on which motion-transfer tools require example motions of the target
character~\citep{chen2025motion2motion}.

\paragraph{Corpus.} The library holds \num{311} rigs, \num{77{,}894} captioned clips and
\num{6.18}M frames at 30\,fps (train \num{73{,}995} / validation \num{3{,}899}), deformation bones only, 34--102 joints per rig (median 64; \num{108} distinct kinematic trees, \num{123} distinct
ordered parent arrays). The Truebones zoo (66
rigs, 986 clips) is never trained on by the backbone; App.~\ref{app:rep} gives the corpus statistics and the conversion.

\subsection{In-context skeleton conditioning}\label{sec:method:model}

\paragraph{Layout.} The network input is $[\,\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{demo}}\mid
\mathbf{x}_t^{\mathrm{tgt}}\,]$ along time: one clean frame holding the rig's analytic rest pose,
never noised, followed by the $T{=}240$ target frames that carry the flow interpolant and are the
only frames denoised and scored. Under the rest-delta parameterisation the rest frame's rotation
channels are the identity on every rig, so it conveys proportions, joint count and graph rather
than motion; after per-cell normalisation it also carries the rig's statistics.

\paragraph{Joint semantics.} Each joint description $d_j$ is embedded once with
LLM2Vec~\citep{behnamghader2024llm2vec} (4096-d), projected and \emph{added} to every token of joint
$j$; descriptions rather than bone names, because ``front left upper leg'' is the same concept on a
horse and a lizard whereas \texttt{L\_Femur\_02} and \texttt{b\_hindleg\_l\_1} are not, and a new
rig's naming scheme need never have been seen.

\paragraph{Structure and graph bias.} An 8-d structural vector per joint (rest-offset direction,
bone length, tree depth, root-path length, child count, leaf flag) enters through a zero-initialised
MLP, and spatial attention receives a fixed bias from the geodesic distance on the tree plus two
learned tables indexed by the hops to the lowest common ancestor (App.~\ref{app:model}).

\paragraph{Block.} Each of the 14 blocks applies temporal attention (tokens are frames, batched
over joints), spatial attention (tokens are joints, batched over frames, with the graph bias) and a
$4\times$ MLP, each modulated by AdaLN~\citep{peebles2023dit} from
$\mathbf{c}=\mathrm{MLP}(t)+\mathrm{MLP}(\mathbf{e}_c)$, with $\mathbf{e}_c$ the pooled LLM2Vec caption
embedding, so text reaches the model only through modulation and the token stream stays kinematic.
Width 896, 14 heads, \num{302.9}M parameters.

\paragraph{Stability and guidance.} Queries and keys are RMS-normalised per head before the dot
product~\citep{dehghani2023vit22b}: in our runs every 0.3B attempt without it diverged with
attention logits growing from $\sim$1.4k to 23k, and with it the eight probes taken through epoch 199
of the \num{303}M run peak at \num{28.1}. The caption is dropped with
probability 0.1 for classifier-free guidance; the rest-pose prompt is never dropped.

\subsection{Calibrated grouped flow-matching objective}\label{sec:method:objective}

\paragraph{Flow matching with $\mathbf{x}$-prediction.} We train on the straight path
$\mathbf{x}_t=(1-t)\,\mathbf{x}_0+t\,\mathbf{x}_1$ with $\mathbf{x}_0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and
$t\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1)$~\citep{lipman2023flow,liu2023rectified}; the network predicts the clean
sample $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1$, and the velocity-space weight of \citet{li2025jit} is applied,
\begin{equation}
  \ell(\mathbf{x}_t,t)=\frac{w(t)}{\mathbb{E}[w]}\;\mathrm{Huber}_{\delta}\!\left(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1-\mathbf{x}_1\right),
  \qquad w(t)=\frac{1}{\max(1-t,\,\sigma_{\min})^{2}},
  \label{eq:elem}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{\min}=0.2$, $\delta=10$ (below the measured 99.99th percentile of the normalised
targets, \num{11.45}; the knee acts on residuals, and at initialisation it binds on \num{0.015}\% of
supervised cells, recorded in the calibration artifact) and a
uniform $t$ sampler (App.~\ref{app:ablation_notes}).

\paragraph{Grouped loss.} A rig with 102 joints has three times the body cells of one with 34 and
the same single root row, so a plain mean over cells would drown the root trajectory, heading and
contact channels---the ones that decide whether a walk is a walk---in body rotations on large rigs.
We partition the channels into nine groups $g$ (root and body $\times$ position, rotation, velocity;
contact; root track; heading) and use
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}=\sum_{g}\gamma_g\sqrt{\frac{N_g}{N}}\;\bar{\ell}_g,
  \label{eq:grouped}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\ell}_g$ is the masked mean of Eq.~\eqref{eq:elem} over the $N_g$ valid cells of group
$g$ and $N=\sum_g N_g$; the groups are the nine channel families of Table~\ref{tab:channels}, weighted to a target share profile~\citep{rempe2026kimodo}.
Under this weighting the explicit $N_g$ dependence of the output-gradient energy of group $g$
cancels: it is proportional to $\gamma_g^{2}Q_g$, where $Q_g$ is the group's mean weighted squared
residual, so a group's weight in $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1$ no longer grows
with how many joints the rig has (App.~\ref{app:calib}). The shares realised during
training still follow the evolving residuals; the weighting removes the joint-count bias, it does not
fix the shares.

\paragraph{Calibration.} The $\gamma_g$ are not hand-tuned. A target share profile over channel
families is pre-registered, the group energies $E_g$ are measured over one pass of the training set
under exactly the training crops and masks, and the weights are solved as
$\gamma=\sqrt{\text{share}/E}$, with the rotation family anchored at $\gamma{=}10$ and the others solved
relative to it. A \emph{mechanism check} then takes 30 optimiser-free steps of a small arm model and verifies that
every family's measured share of $\|\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1\|^{2}$, accumulated
over those steps, matches the $\gamma_g^{2}Q_g$ prediction within 25\%. It certifies the wiring of the
weights, not that the pre-registered profile is realised during training: the realised share moves with
the error energies, which evolve. The trainer refuses to start unless the artifact matches the objective
it is run with ($t$ sampler, $\sigma_{\min}$, Huber knee, masks, batch size; App.~\ref{app:calib}).

\paragraph{Auxiliary terms.} A forward-kinematics consistency term between predicted positions and
positions forward-kinematised from predicted rotations, a velocity term on world joint displacements,
a foot-lock term gated by the contact channel, and an acceleration-matching term are added in the same
frame (App.~\ref{app:calib}).

\paragraph{Optimisation.} AdamW, learning rate $1.5\times10^{-4}$ at global batch 128 (the
\num{36}M pilot: $2\times10^{-4}$), 4k warm-up steps, cosine decay to a $10^{-2}$ floor over 40
epochs, bf16, gradient clipping at 1.0, and rejection of any step whose pre-clipping gradient norm
exceeds $200$; 8 H200 GPUs, \num{578} steps per epoch, \num{290} epochs (best validation loss at 289).

\subsection{Sampling}\label{sec:method:sampling}
Samples follow the straight path with Euler steps
$\mathbf{x}\leftarrow\mathbf{x}+(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1-\mathbf{x})/(S-i)$, $S{=}20$; invalid cells are
projected back to zero after every step, and guidance is applied on the text stream only,
$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1=\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1^{\varnothing}+s\,(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1^{c}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_1^{\varnothing})$
with $s{=}2$. Twenty steps is the operating point used throughout; ten give the same retrieval at half the
cost, and App.~\ref{app:cost} gives the latency and quality of both.

\subsection{Extending the model to a rig it has never seen}\label{sec:method:lora}
\paragraph{Zero-training arm.} An unseen rig is served by the frozen backbone as soon as its rest
pose, joint descriptions and per-cell statistics (from its own clips) are supplied; on the Truebones
rigs this yields motion with far more jitter than the real clips, since wing chains and eight-legged
gaits are absent from the training prior.

\paragraph{Per-rig LoRA.} When roughly ten clips of the new rig exist, we attach rank-64 or rank-128
adapters~\citep{hu2022lora} (scale 1) to the attention projections, the MLPs and the conditioning
paths (joint-semantics projection, caption MLP, structural MLP, AdaLN), leaving the input and output
projections and the timestep MLP frozen; the adapters hold 26.8M (r64) or 53.6M (r128) parameters
and are folded back into the weights at export, so every consumer loads a plain checkpoint. The
new rig's data view is built by the corpus script (joint pruning to the deformation-bone convention,
corpus-style captions, a split by source animation) and the adapter is trained with the backbone
recipe for 300--900 epochs (App.~\ref{app:lora}); a single adapter shared by a group of rigs
recovers most of the same ground (App.~\ref{app:extend_more}).

\subsection{Skeleton-aware evaluation}\label{sec:method:eval}
\paragraph{Evaluator.} Retrieval metrics require a text--motion embedding, and existing ones are
tied to one skeleton. We train two towers into a shared 512-d space: a frozen DistilBERT text tower
with a trainable pooling head, and a graph-aware motion tower (6 graph layers, 4 temporal layers)
over the \rep{} channels without contact, with a symmetric InfoNCE objective whose false-negative
mask ties together clips of the same motion, the same source animation and the same caption. The
evaluator sees only real data, never generated samples, and shares no weights with the generator.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Text$\to$motion R@1/2/3 in pools of 64, matching score and FID in the
evaluator space, each next to the evaluator's score on the real clips of the same pools (with shuffled
captions the evaluator scores R@1 \num{0.035} in pools of 32, chance \num{0.031}); generation is fixed at 20 steps,
$s{=}2$ and one seed and sharded across GPUs under a hashed generation plan with provenance checks.
Rigs outside the library, for which no evaluator exists, are scored geometrically
(\S\ref{sec:exp:setup}).
