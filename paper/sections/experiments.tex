\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:exp:setup}

\paragraph{Library and splits.} The training library is a \num{311}-rig animal corpus in \rep{}
(\num{77{,}894} clips, \num{6.18}M frames at 30\,fps; deformation bones only, 34--102 joints per rig, median 64,
\num{108} distinct kinematic trees); validation holds \num{3{,}899} clips, \num{5}\% of every rig's
(App.~\ref{app:split}). Rigs outside the library come from
the Truebones zoo~\citep{truebones}: \num{986} clips over \num{66} rigs converted to \rep{} with the same
pipeline (\num{802} captioned), each rig split $80/20$ into train and validation clips. Per-cell statistics are
those of all of a rig's clips (App.~\ref{app:rep}); Table~\ref{tab:supportonly} uses the adaptation clips alone.

\paragraph{Models and optimisation.} The main model has $N_b{=}14$ blocks of width $D{=}896$ with $H{=}14$
heads (\num{302.9}M parameters); the \num{36}M pilot ($N_b{=}8$, $D{=}384$, $H{=}6$) shares every other setting
except learning rate and length of training, and is used for the ablations. Both train with AdamW---warm-up,
cosine decay, gradient clipping and rejection of any step whose pre-clipping gradient norm exceeds a
threshold---in bf16; every symbol of \S\ref{sec:method} has its value in Table~\ref{tab:hparams}
(App.~\ref{app:training}). The checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is the one scored throughout.


\paragraph{Evaluator.} Retrieval metrics require a text--motion embedding, and existing ones are tied to one
skeleton. We train two towers into a shared embedding space: a frozen DistilBERT text tower with a trainable
pooling head and a graph-aware motion tower over the \rep{} channels excluding the contact flag, with a symmetric
InfoNCE objective (App.~\ref{app:split}). It sees only real data, never generated samples, shares no weights with
the generator, and scores at chance when the captions are shuffled.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Generation uses $S{=}20$ Euler steps with guidance scale $s{=}2$ everywhere, at the
length of the real clip (at most $L{=}240$ frames); Table~\ref{tab:main} is the mean of five sampling seeds and every other table uses seed 42 (the Truebones arms:
App.~\ref{app:lora}). Retrieval metrics are text-to-motion R@1/2/3 in
pools of 64 formed by chunking the dataset order (a retrieved motion is correct if it is the caption's own
clip), the FID on the evaluator's $\ell_2$-normalised embeddings and the text--motion matching score, each
next to the evaluator's score on the real clips of the same pools. The evaluator
scores the sampled channels; re-scored on the played animation (positions and velocities from the forward kinematics
of the predicted rotations), R@1 moves by $+0.001$ at \num{303}M and $-0.003$ at \num{36}M (App.~\ref{app:metrics}). Geometry is measured in world space on that
deployable output. The \emph{FK--pose gap} is the mean distance, in the rig's mean bone length, between the
forward-kinematised and the directly predicted joint positions: a consistency of the two output channels, not a
distance to the target. On Truebones rigs, for which no skeleton-aware evaluator exists, articulation, jitter and root speed
are reported as ratios to the rig's real clips (App.~\ref{app:metrics}).

\subsection{One model for 311 skeletons}\label{sec:exp:main}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports text-to-motion retrieval on the validation set. All \num{311} rigs appear in
training, so this measures the generation of held-out clips on known rigs; rigs outside the library are
the subject of \S\ref{sec:exp:extend}. The \num{303}M model reaches R@1 \num{0.867}, matching score
\num{0.764}, FID \num{0.005} and Diversity \num{1.400}, where the real clips score \num{0.904}, \num{0.782} and
\num{1.399} under the same evaluator in the same pools; the \num{36}M pilot reaches R@1 \num{0.840} and FID
\num{0.008}. R-precision uses the \num{3{,}840} clips of the 60 complete pools; the other columns use all
\num{3{,}899}. One sequence takes \num{4.1}\,s at the protocol's 20 steps on one H200 (batch 1, fp32, 240 padded
frames) and \num{0.69}\,s for the pilot; halving the steps leaves R@1 unchanged (\num{0.863} against \num{0.864})
for \num{0.003} of FID (\num{0.005} for the pilot), and five steps cost \num{0.008} of R@1 (Table~\ref{tab:cost}).

\subsection{Extending the model to rigs it has never seen}\label{sec:exp:extend}

\paragraph{Zero training and per-rig LoRA.} We take four Truebones rigs that differ from the library in body
plan and joint count (Table~\ref{tab:lora}) and compare three arms on the same held-out clips of each: (a) the library model with the new rig's per-cell
statistics as its only rig-specific input
(\emph{zero training}); (b) rank-$r$ adapters~\citep{hu2022lora} with scale $\alpha$ on the attention
projections, the MLPs and the conditioning paths, trained with the backbone recipe on the rig's 8--26 training clips
and folded back into the weights at export (\emph{LoRA}; App.~\ref{app:lora}); (c) a \num{36}M model of the same recipe trained on all \num{802}
captioned Truebones clips, evaluation clips included (\emph{TB-only}). Table~\ref{tab:lora} gives the
FK--pose gap before and after adaptation next to the articulation and jitter of the same sampled output: eight to
twenty-six clips bring the gap from $0.57$--$4.5$ bone lengths to $0.23$--$0.35$, with the largest drop on the two
rigs farthest from the library, and the TB-only reference sits at $0.08$--$0.21$; the adapted output's
articulation and jitter ratios are $0.24$--$0.77$ and $0.20$--$0.86$ (App.~\ref{app:extend_more}). The jitter
excess is gone within 50--100 adapter epochs (\num{50}--\num{300} optimiser steps on the 8--26 clips), and one
rank-128 adapter pooled over eight flying rigs removes it on all seven scored rigs (median jitter ratio \num{5.5} to
\num{0.99}; Tables~\ref{tab:lora_len} and~\ref{tab:flyers}). With
statistics from the adaptation clips alone, the gap is $0.25$--$0.36$ and the jitter excess is gone
(Table~\ref{tab:supportonly}); Figure~\ref{fig:tb_main}
shows the adapted model on the four rigs. The released AnyTop
weights were trained on the whole Truebones zoo, including these four rigs; App.~\ref{app:anytop} reports its
per-rig geometry beside ours as an in-distribution reference.

\subsection{Ablations}\label{sec:exp:ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:simple} removes the ingredients of \S\ref{sec:method:model} and \S\ref{sec:method:objective}
one at a time from the \num{36}M pilot and adds the flat construction by which fixed-skeleton generators are
usually brought to variable skeletons (App.~\ref{app:flat}; same recipe and budget, \num{5}\% more parameters). The calibrated group
weights are worth \num{0.036}--\num{0.043} of R@1; the joint descriptions none on their own; removing the descriptions
together with the geodesic and direction biases and the structural features costs a further
\num{0.029}--\num{0.033}; and flattening the joint axis costs \num{0.192}--\num{0.201} and multiplies the FID by
\num{3.7}. A 64-frame motion clip of the same rig in the demonstration slot, in place of the rest frame, is equal
in retrieval (R@1 \num{0.822} against \num{0.821}) and worse where the two representations of a pose have to agree
(FK--pose gap \num{0.202} against \num{0.171} bone lengths), so we keep the rest frame (App.~\ref{app:demo64}).

Table~\ref{tab:geom} measures, on the deployable output, what the representation buys together with the two
world-coordinate loss terms written on its channels, which the 13-channel arm trains without
(App.~\ref{app:ablation_notes}). The 13-channel AnyTop
layout derived from the same clips is within a point of R@1 of \rep{} at every matched epoch, and within
\num{0.008} once its objective and partition are matched (App.~\ref{app:ablation_notes},
Table~\ref{tab:repcontent}): retrieval does not resolve the layout. Geometry does. The two output channels of a
\rep{} sample agree to \num{0.38} bone lengths against \num{1.45} at \num{36}M (\num{0.24} at \num{303}M); its
leaf joints articulate, whereas the 13-channel layout has no rotation slot for them and the conversion fills it
from the parent; its feet slide less while flagged in contact; and its root travels within \num{4}\% of the
data's displacement at \num{36}M (\num{10}\% at \num{303}M), against \num{22}\% further for the layout that
integrates a root velocity. Per-cell statistics
are worth about two points of R@1 and \num{22}--\num{30}\% of the FID over a per-rig scale (Table~\ref{tab:rep}); at
matched optimiser steps the \num{36}M, \num{116}M and \num{303}M models reach R@1 \num{0.821}, \num{0.843} and
\num{0.857} (Table~\ref{tab:size}).
