\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Text-to-motion generation has made striking progress, but almost all of it on one body. The standard
pipeline---a diffusion model, a motion tokenizer or a latent generator trained on
HumanML3D~\citep{guo2022generating}---assumes a fixed kinematic tree, typically the 22-joint SMPL
skeleton, and bakes it into every layer: the input projection is sized for that joint count and any
codebook is learned for one morphology. The result is a
family of models that animate a human well and cannot animate anything else.

\method{} (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}) drops that assumption: a rig is data, not architecture.
The applications that most need generative motion---games, film previsualisation, virtual
production---are populated by characters that are not human, and a studio does not have one skeleton
but hundreds, each authored by a rigger with its own joint count, naming scheme and helper bones. Our corpus is a faithful sample of that reality: \num{311} production animal rigs
with \num{34} to \num{102} deformation joints (median 64), \num{108} distinct kinematic trees and
\num{77{,}894} captioned clips (\num{6.2}M frames).\footnote{The clips and their captions follow the
AniMo4D annotation set of \citet{wang2025animo}; the rigs are re-exported with deformation bones only,
the joint descriptions are ours, and the conversion to \rep{} is new (App.~\ref{app:rep}). The Truebones
zoo, a publicly distributed set of 66 animal rigs, is kept entirely outside training.} Generating on a canonical skeleton and
\emph{retargeting}~\citep{villegas2018nkn,aberman2020skeleton,lee2023same} degrades as morphology
diverges, and a model per character multiplies data and compute by the size of the cast; the motion
has to be generated for the target body, and it has to come out as rotations the rig can play.

\paragraph{Our position.} A generative model should treat the skeleton as \emph{input}, not as
architecture: told the kinematic tree, the rest pose and what each joint \emph{is}, the network needs
no per-skeleton parameter, and adding a character becomes a data question. The interface is due to
AnyTop~\citep{gat2025anytop}, which prepends the rest pose and embeds joint names to generate, without
text, for the Truebones zoo. Text-conditioned generation across skeletons has since
been shown by OmniZoo~\citep{chen2025omnizoo} on 140 species, by G-MDM~\citep{lee2025dragon} on
Truebones with joint rotations but no root translation, and by SAMoR~\citep{samor2026}, which pools
any skeleton into eight part tokens over joint positions and recovers rotations by inverse
kinematics. Building on skeleton-conditioned generation, we study joint-space rotation and
root-trajectory prediction across \num{311} production rigs at 0.3B parameters, with controlled
representation experiments and an external-rig adaptation study; the output is what a rig plays,
per-joint rotations and a world-space root trajectory, with no inverse-kinematics fit.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{\rep{}, a per-joint representation with per-cell normalisation}
  (\S\ref{sec:method:rep}). Every joint carries the same 17 channels---position, rotation as a 6D
  delta from the rest pose, velocity, contact, plus a root-only world trajectory and heading---and
  every (rig, joint, channel) cell is standardised by its own statistics. Any rigged animation
  converts to it from its kinematic tree, rest pose and motion, with no template, retargeting,
  tokenizer or inverse kinematics. Standardising per cell, rather than per rig, raises R@1 by 2.0--2.1
  points and lowers the FID by 22--30\% at each of three matched epochs; against the 13-channel AnyTop
  layout derived from the same clips, the layout with its world-coordinate loss terms keeps leaf-joint
  articulation, foot contact and root travel at equal retrieval (\S\ref{sec:exp:ablation}).
\item \textbf{In-context skeleton conditioning} (\S\ref{sec:method:model}). The rest pose is
  prepended as one clean frame, natural-language descriptions of the joints (``left front lower
  leg'', ``tail segment 3'') are added to their tokens, and spatial attention is biased by tree
  distance. The network holds no per-skeleton parameter: joints occupy positions in a learned table
  of 160 slots in the order the rig lists them, and what a slot \emph{is} comes from its
  description, so a new rig is a new set of inputs.
\item \textbf{A calibrated grouped flow-matching objective and a recipe that trains at 0.3B}
  (\S\ref{sec:method:objective}). Channel groups are weighted from measured group energies so that
  the root, contact and trajectory channels of a large rig are not drowned by its many body
  rotations; a mechanism check certifies the calibration before training; query--key normalisation
  makes the 0.3B model trainable; and retrieval and FID improve monotonically from \num{36}M to
  \num{303}M parameters at a matched budget.
\item \textbf{An extension study} (\S\ref{sec:method:lora}, \S\ref{sec:exp:extend}). A rig outside
  the library is served by the same forward pass given its rest pose, joint descriptions and per-cell
  statistics, and a per-rig low-rank adapter trained on eight to twenty-six clips brings its rotation
  and position channels into agreement on bodies far from the training distribution (a winged
  dragon, an eight-legged spider); one adapter shared across eight flying rigs recovers most of that on rigs
  with no adapter of their own.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Results.} One \num{303}M-parameter \method{} trained on all \num{311} rigs is scored by a
skeleton-aware text--motion evaluator on \num{3{,}899} held-out clips of those rigs: R@1 \num{0.922}
against \num{0.964} for the real clips in the same pools, FID \num{0.005} (\S\ref{sec:exp:main}); the
\num{36}M pilot reaches \num{0.897}. On Truebones rigs never seen in training, a per-rig adapter on
ten to fifteen clips lowers the FK--pose gap (the disagreement between the rotation and position
channels of the prediction) from \num{1.215} to \num{0.146} bone lengths on a 95-joint dragon, while
the adapted outputs move less than the real clips.
